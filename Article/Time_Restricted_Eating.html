Summary

Overconsumption of carbohydrate-rich food combined with adverse eating patterns contributes to metabolic syndrome. This randomized trial compared low-carbohydrate diet (LCD), 8-hour time-restricted eating (TRE), and combined intervention for effects on body weight, abdominal fat, and cardiometabolic outcomes.

Compared with baseline, all interventions reduced body weight and subcutaneous fat. TRE and combination treatment additionally reduced visceral fat, fasting blood glucose, uric acid and dyslipidemia. Combination intervention induced the greatest weight loss.

Introduction

Metabolic syndrome is characterized by abdominal obesity, elevated blood pressure, fasting blood glucose and atherogenic dyslipidemia, increasing risks of diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Lifestyle interventions remain first-line therapy.

Among dietary interventions, low-carbohydrate diets and time-restricted eating have shown promise. This trial investigates their relative and combined effects in adults with metabolic syndrome.

Discussion

TRE yielded greater benefits than LCD alone for visceral obesity and cardiometabolic outcomes. Combination treatment generated clinically significant weight loss approaching 5.8%.

Visceral fat, rather than subcutaneous fat, correlated strongly with several cardiometabolic parameters. The findings support TRE, with or without LCD, as an effective intervention for metabolic syndrome.
